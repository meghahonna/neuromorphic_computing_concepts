Three Things Happening Every Timestep

1

Leak — the drain pulls water out

Every timestep, the membrane potential is multiplied by β. If β = 0.85, the neuron retains 85% of its charge and loses 15%. This is why it's called "leaky" — without new input, the potential always decays toward zero. A high β (0.95) means long memory. A low β (0.5) means the neuron forgets fast.

2

Integrate — inputs pour water in

After the leak, incoming signals (weighted inputs from other neurons) are added to the membrane potential. Multiple inputs arriving close together accumulate — their water piles up faster than the drain can remove it. This is temporal integration.

3

Fire — water hits the overflow line

If the membrane potential crosses the threshold, the neuron emits a spike — a brief electrical pulse sent to all downstream neurons. Then the potential resets to zero. This "all-or-nothing" firing is what makes spiking neural networks fundamentally different from traditional neural networks, where every neuron outputs a continuous value every time.

V[t] = β · V[t-1] + I[t]
V[t] = membrane potential now  |  β = decay rate (leak)  |  V[t-1] = potential last step  |  I[t] = input current
If V[t] ≥ threshold → FIRE spike, then V[t] = 0

Why Time Matters

Try this in the simulator above: send two spikes close together (press SPACE quickly twice). The second spike builds on what's left from the first — the membrane potential climbs higher. Now wait 5 seconds and send two more spikes with a long gap between them. The first one leaks away before the second arrives — no accumulation.

This is the core insight: a LIF neuron is a temporal coincidence detector. It responds strongly when inputs are correlated in time and weakly when they're spread out. Traditional neural networks have no concept of this — they see all inputs simultaneously.

Connecting to 3-Way Matching

Gradual Inflation Fraud

A vendor slowly increases invoice amounts by 1-2% each month. Each invoice individually looks fine — it's within the 2% threshold. A rule-based system passes every single one.

But in the LIF neuron: each slightly-inflated invoice adds a small amount to the membrane potential on the "amount deviation" channel. With a high β (slow leak), this charge accumulates over weeks. Drip... drip... drip. Eventually the potential crosses threshold and the neuron fires — flagging a pattern that no individual check could catch.

The membrane potential IS the memory. It's the neuron's running sense of "something isn't right here" — too subtle for a threshold check, too gradual for a human auditor, but the leaky integration catches it.

Phantom Vendor

A new vendor appears with no transaction history and immediately submits invoices. In a normal vendor, the neuron has built a baseline — it knows the "rhythm." For this vendor, there IS no baseline.

Every single input lands on a neuron with no established decay pattern. The membrane potential spikes immediately because there's no learned normal to absorb it against. Maximum firing rate from day one.

Invoice Before Goods Receipt

The temporal sequence should always be: PO spike → Receipt spike → Invoice spike. In the SNN, the channels are literally ordered in time. When an invoice spike arrives BEFORE the receipt spike, the temporal ordering of the spike train is reversed — the neuron processes the channels in the wrong sequence.

This isn't a rule someone wrote. The network learned the normal temporal pattern during training, and a reversed sequence produces a completely different membrane potential trajectory. The anomaly is detected natively by the physics of the computation.

Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP)

If the LIF neuron is how neurons compute, STDP is how they learn. The rule is simple: neurons that fire together, wire together — but the order matters.

Δw = weight change  |  η = learning rate  |  Δt = time between spikes  |  τ = time constant
Closer in time → bigger change  |  Far apart → almost no change

Putting It All Together

LIF neurons give the network temporal memory — the ability to accumulate evidence over time through the membrane potential. STDP gives the network temporal learning — the ability to discover which sequences of events matter by strengthening connections between neurons that fire in meaningful patterns.

Together in your 3-way matching engine: the SNN learns the normal rhythm of PO → Receipt → Invoice for each vendor. The LIF neurons' membrane potentials track the ongoing "health" of each transaction stream. STDP reinforces the synaptic pathways that encode normal behavior. When fraud breaks the pattern, the membrane potential trajectory diverges from what the network has learned — and it fires.
